Cockpit design system

Everything below is rendered by the same code that draws the product, not pictured. The marks, plates, controls and layouts on this page are live; if a rule and its specimen disagree, the specimen is telling the truth. Written from the locked direction before the build, which means every value is a decision rather than a measurement until the finish review confirms it.

The world

You look out, not down. Cockpit is a canopy onto the corpus: the second brain is a volume of indexed knowledge inside a geodesic cage, every particle in it swimming, and the interface is a small number of hard-edged instrument plates floating in your line of sight. You can turn the volume with your hand. Pressing it flattens it into a map you can sort.

This refuses the category default it replaces — the agentic-OS panel grid on a dark canvas, which was built, committed, and discarded. In the panel grid the corpus is content inside a widget. Here the corpus is the ground you are looking at, and the widgets are instruments over it.

Two kinds of matter. Inside, in colour: one node per indexed file — 583 across Vault, Skills and The Brewery. Outside, in mono: the seven efforts, riding vertices of the cage. Those are repos Cockpit launches into but does not index. You can see at a glance what the product knows and what it merely runs.
Every node must resolve to a real file. The moment a node is drawn that corresponds to nothing, the field is wallpaper and must be deleted rather than tuned.

Colour

The shell is achromatic. The second brain is not, and it is the only thing that is not.

The cluster palette

Seven hues at one mid-luminance band, so no cluster dominates and each reads on both grounds. Colour appears in exactly two places — the nodes themselves, and the legend that names the clusters. Not in plates, not in buttons, not in state, not in icons.

Node marks

A node encodes three things at once and only one of them is colour: shape is type, fill is trust, colour is cluster. This is why the field reads as a corpus rather than as generic particles.

The five types are the vault's own closed vocabulary, read from .vault-standard.yml — not invented, and not extendable. The standard says in bold "Do not invent a sixth."

Fill is the payload. Filled means endorsed, operative or owned. Hollow means captured but not claimed — "cite or don't claim" made visible. Of 61 typed notes, 41 are resource against 7 concept. The honest picture is a corpus heavy with captured material and light on original claims, and the design shows that rather than flattering it.

Size regimes

Below 1.6px every mark collapses to a plain dot. At rest the volume is texture and nobody is reading individual types; shape resolves as nodes grow. A shape nobody can see is not information.

Each mark draws in two passes: an additive bloom at 3.4 × r and 0.30 α so marks sit in light rather than on flat black, then the crisp mark. Filled marks above 2.6px take a specular at (−0.34r, −0.38r). Stubs render at 0.45 α.

The active run is the Hub's visual peak. The Workspace Explorer occupies the top of the leading sidebar. Its lower WAYS TO WORK section holds all seven Recipes in their fixed order and selects one causal plate rather than turning the screen into a dashboard. The Stack is durable configuration at the trailing edge. Slot identity is glyph, label, position, and the bounded agent rim.
The recipe makes its guarantee visible in the stage strip: separate answers for Explore, a held release before Synthesize writes, dependency work for Coordinate, and gate-first work for Validate. Streams show only observed telemetry; unavailable data stays unavailable.
Agent rims identify vendors, never outcomes. Codex is blue, Claude is orange, and AGY carries a rainbow 2px rim. Running, done, failed, held, queued, and wants-you continue to read through glyph and label, whether or not a user can distinguish colour.

Loop

Two opposed Wires between the same pair derive one Loop: two lanes, 1px braces, and a badge. Detach either leg and the survivor becomes a plain Wire. The cap is five fix rounds, with one free round only when the acceptance gate itself is repaired.

Controls

The controller is achromatic. Colour is pinned to the corpus, and a controller is chrome, so the active state is a full inversion rather than an accent hue. These are live — drag them.

Every control moves real nodes. There are no painted controls in this design. A control that does nothing is a lie the rest of the product would have to keep.
Settings persist to config. A folder move must not be a code change, and that applies to appearance too.

Icons

The Fragment family, chosen against five alternates. Authored SVG only — no icon library, no unicode glyphs, no emoji. 14px viewBox, stroke-width 1.4, angular with cut corners, the same chamfer language as the plates.

Any new icon is drawn into this family by hand. Reaching for Lucide or Heroicons for "just one missing icon" is the specific failure this rule exists to prevent.

Layer 0 layouts

A 3D rest state and four flat layouts. Every node carries all five coordinate sets and interpolates between them — nothing is spawned or destroyed at any boundary, which is what makes every transition continuity rather than a cut.

Circle is the one layout that ranks the corpus rather than just arranging it, and it ranks it into exactly two bands. Skills is the inner ring, because it is what the OS actually runs on — the vault types skills as reference, "a procedure consulted and followed — operative". Everything else shares one outer ring, divided into contiguous arcs sized by count so each cluster keeps its identity and its label. Seven separate rings were built first and diluted the claim into a gradient; two rings state it.

Vault sentinel

The one object at the true centre of layer 0 that is not a file. It is the destination everything links to, not a body of files, so it should read as a mechanism and not a population.

A gimbal: three chamfered rings on three independent axes, foreshortened by the same three noise streams that drive the rest-state tumble, with bearing pins where each ring meets its axis and a solid chamfered core. Chosen against an iris of concentric segment rings (too close to the cluster family — risked reading as the biggest cluster) and a static keystone block (the calmest read, but least related to the corpus it sits inside).

There is no warm mark in the shell. The gimbal replaces an earlier orange hexagon — the one exception the achromatic-shell rule used to carry. That exception is spent.
It never recedes. Pressing a cluster anchor dims every other cluster toward the Neighborhood, the way the legend already mutes clusters — but the sentinel holds full weight and turns its face, a hard triangular pointer, toward whichever anchor is pressed. It is where the work ends up, so it is the one thing that does not fade. Not yet built — recorded ahead of it.

Cluster anchors

The seven cluster anchors in the Explorer are painted in the field, not a DOM element layered over the canvas. A CSS clip-path octagon was tried first and read as a flat image, with none of the world's depth, light or noise.

A shell is six segments on a ring, each an annular quad separated by a gap and sliding on its own value-noise stream by about ±3.5%, around a chamfered square nucleus. Limbs leave the shell toward its real link directions — straight, with one elbow and a terminal tick, routed rather than grown. Segment count is fixed at six; only the shell radius reads file count, so an 8-file cluster and a 3699-file cluster are the same object at different scale. Below about 5px the segments merge into a solid chamfered hexagon — the same honest collapse the corpus marks make below 1.6px.

Rigid, not organic. A noise-deformed membrane version was built first and rejected: it read as biology, and this world is machined. Life comes from articulation — joints flex, segments slide on their tracks, the body yaws — never from deformation, and never from a pulse.
Pressing one collapses it inward over roughly 620ms: the shell contracts and its limbs retract, its member nodes pull in, the aperture opens to take them, and the Neighborhood resolves from the shell's own position. Reverse runs faster (~440ms) — it regroups faster than it released.

Motion

Binding, and the difference between a live organism and a screensaver. Everything is driven by value noise over a 512-entry table. Both traces below show 40 seconds of real signal.

ChannelDriveRule
Particle swim3 streams per nodeThis is the life. Every particle wanders in X, Y and Z at its own tempo, so the volume is never still and never moves as one body
Tumble3 signed streams, X/Y/ZIndependent, so they never re-align and no orientation recurs. A single-axis constant spin is explicitly wrong
Wandersigned, scaled by spinFlat layouts drift, stall and reverse
Removed deliberately: global breath, cluster pulse, alpha twinkle. All three were built and all three are gone. A radius that swells and clusters that beat read as one organism flexing; the corpus is not one organism, it is 583 things. Blinking is pulsing by another name. Life here is displacement, not brightness.
Under prefers-reduced-motion all three channels stop dead. The field is completely still and fully legible.

Input

The volume is a role="button" that is also a trackball. Both gestures live on one surface, so the disambiguation is part of the spec rather than an implementation detail.

Owed & unwritten

The field of fragments — owed

The world is named the fragment field, and the mono debris that named it is currently absent: it was in the first pass and did not survive the layer 0 rework. It returns in the final design as the background ground. This is a commitment, not an idea.

One question to settle first: the design already assigns meaning by position, so a third population needs its own honest referent or it becomes wallpaper. The standing answer is unindexed working matter — repo trees and vendored dependencies, the 115,215 files under Studybuddy's FrontEnd.

The light theme — open

Both themes are first-class and designed, not inverted. The light theme is not yet designed, and a naive inversion of a black field with a lit volume will not work. It is a design task, not a token flip.

Console state extension — decided

The five-state Console vocabulary is now decided: Running, Done, Failed, Wants you, and Idle. State remains glyph-plus-label first; the light theme remains open.

Extract findings

Consolidated in documentation: the Hub reuses --ink, --ink-dim, --ink-idle, --plate, --plate-edge, --tick, --chamfer, --mono, and --disp. Left deliberately duplicated: Hub wire geometry, Loop braces, and card-size specimens are surface-specific and do not yet recur three times with the same intent. No new runtime token or wrapper is warranted.
